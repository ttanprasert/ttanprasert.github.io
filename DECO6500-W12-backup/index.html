What to do at your step

Phase 1 · Design your method · aim: by today

  1. Step 1 Choose your question. A Band 3 or 4 question about your A2 system, and the point in the system it’s about. Use your Week 10 notes and From Questions to Method. Done when: you can say your question and where in the system it’s about
  2. Step 2 Find the mechanism. What makes the change happen at that point: people’s behaviour, social practices, or how a system or organisation performs. See From Questions to Method. Done when: you can finish “At [that point], [this] happens because [who or what] does [what]”
  3. Step 3 Choose a lens. A theory that explains your mechanism. Its parts are the topics your evidence must cover. Find a starting lens with Decision Guide step 3 in the A3 Method Guide. Done when: you can name your lens and list its parts
  4. Step 4 Adapt a futuring technique. So your method collects evidence one step into the future, grounded in more than speculation. See the four ways in From Questions to Method and the techniques in the A3 Method Guide. Done when: your method reaches Year 1 or Year 3, and each future rests on a document, a case or a person
  5. Step 5 Design your method. Two checks. Breadth: does it collect evidence on every part of your lens? Future: take the future out. Does your evidence change? It should. The two-deletion test is in From Questions to Method, between steps 5 and 6. Done when: you can say your method in one sentence, and it passes both checks
  6. Step 6 · evaluation path Set your yardstick. Three or four method evaluation criteria, each finishing “…which I will know because…”, and your strongest claim: what your method could show if everything went to plan, and what it couldn’t. Examples are in the Week 12 lecture slides and lecture worksheet. Done when: they’re in a dated entry in your working log, before you run anything

Phase 2 · Run it · before Friday 30 October

  1. Step 7 Get it checked. Self-check first (below), then let your tutor know you’re ready. They’ll talk it through with you for five to eight minutes and give you two or three points of feedback. Then get your materials ready, sort out consent, and recruit. Done when: your tutor has checked it and you’ve written their feedback, and what you’ll change, in your log
  2. Step 8 · with run notes Run it, and keep run notes. Do your method’s steps once, from start to end. Keep short, dated notes: who took part, what changed, and every AI output you used and how you checked it. Try your scenario or artefact on a classmate first, and note what changed. Done when: your method has run once, and your run notes are up to date

A small run is enough

A3 asks you to evaluate your method. A small run, evaluated honestly against the criteria you set, can still lead to a strong report. Do your method’s steps once, from start to end, at a small scale: two or three participants; one short workshop; two or three people over three to five days for an SMS scenario study; or about five to eight sources for a desk-based method.

Before you ask for a check, can you say…

  • Your A2 intervention and your question, in one sentence?
  • Your mechanism, and which part of it your lens explains?
  • Which evidence covers each part of your lens?
  • What changes if you take the future out?
  • Your criteria and strongest claim, and when you logged them?
  • Who takes part, and what you’ll tell them?

A check is feedback, not a pass or fail. Write the feedback in your log, with what you’ll change or why you won’t; then you can start running. If your participants are under 18 or in a vulnerable group, your tutor passes your method to Pete first.

Still no clear method? Pick a quick route

If you’re in Phase 1 without a clear method by the break, choose one of these. Each can be run in about a week. Choose by your mechanism (step 2), not by what seems easiest.

  1. Short workshop Three to five people, about 60 minutes, with one futuring technique run in the room (a Futures Wheel, or Year 1, 3 and 5). Fits social mechanisms: how groups work together. Classmates can take part, but can’t stand in for perspectives they don’t have.
  2. Scenario study by SMS Over three to five days, two or three people get short scenario messages (“It’s 2028 and … is in place. What do you do this morning?”) and reply from their daily lives. Fits behaviour mechanisms. Start by Monday 26 October; phone numbers only with consent; de-identify replies and delete messages afterwards.
  3. Desk-based Five to eight sources (policy, reports, comparable cases, studies) read through your lens, then a futuring technique where every future claim names its source. Fits questions about policy, organisations or comparable cases. No first-hand perspectives: say so in Section 6.

Before you leave: log entry 3

A dated entry in your working log, whatever step you’re on

  • What have I decided? Your method, or the quick route you chose.
  • What did I consider instead? The alternative you set aside, and why.
  • What am I unsure about? What could stop your run, and your fallback.
  • What has changed, and why? Your tutor’s feedback, and what you’ll change (or why not).

Add your run dates. Keep it in your UQ OneDrive, and never edit an earlier entry.
